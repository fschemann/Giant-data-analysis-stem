Resources

This page collects the open resources referenced throughout the module, plus the practice dataset used in Rubrics 1–4. Everything here is either open-access or explicitly licensed for reuse — check each source’s own licence before adapting it.

Practice Dataset

The worked example used across Chapter 1 through Chapter 4 is real, open climate data from the Deutscher Wetterdienst (DWD, Germany’s national meteorological service): annual regional averages of air temperature, precipitation and sunshine duration for five German Bundesländer (Bayern, Sachsen, Nordrhein-Westfalen, Schleswig-Holstein, Baden-Württemberg) plus the national “Deutschland” series, covering 1991–2024 (34 years — long enough to see a real, substantial warming trend). Source: DWD Climate Data Center, regional averages, licensed CC BY 4.0. data/raw/dwd_climate_raw.csv (the real DWD values, with a small number of disclosed, deliberately reintroduced formatting issues for Chapter 1’s data-quality exercises) and data/clean/dwd_climate_clean.csv (tidy, analysis-ready). Full column definitions, exact source URLs and a full disclosure of exactly what was changed for teaching purposes are in data/README.md in the project folder. Regenerate the dataset with data/generate_climate_dataset.py.

About the Blueprint

This module’s structure and tone take inspiration from Christina Bogner’s Environmental Statistics lecture notes (Bookdown, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) — numbered chapters building from data basics through modelling, worked exercises throughout and an appendix of further resources. ## Practice Dataset

The worked example used across [Chapter 1](01-understanding-data.qmd) through
[Chapter 4](04-statistical-modelling.qmd) is **real, open climate data** from the
Deutscher Wetterdienst (DWD, Germany's national meteorological service): annual
regional averages of air temperature, precipitation and sunshine duration for five
German Bundesländer (Bayern, Sachsen, Nordrhein-Westfalen, Schleswig-Holstein,
Baden-Württemberg) plus the national "Deutschland" series, covering 1991–2024 (34
years — long enough to see a real, substantial warming trend). Source:
[DWD Climate Data Center, regional averages](https://opendata.dwd.de/climate_environment/CDC/regional_averages_DE/annual/),
licensed **CC BY 4.0**. `data/raw/dwd_climate_raw.csv` (the real DWD values, with a
small number of disclosed, deliberately reintroduced formatting issues for Chapter 1's
data-quality exercises) and `data/clean/dwd_climate_clean.csv` (tidy, analysis-ready).
Full column definitions, exact source URLs and a full disclosure of exactly what was
changed for teaching purposes are in `data/README.md` in the project folder. Regenerate
the dataset with `data/generate_climate_dataset.py`.
